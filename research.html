Reducing Mental-Health Stigma and Encouraging Help-Seeking: A Randomized Online Intervention in Spain

with Antonio Cabrales, Maria Cubel, Santiago Sánchez Pagés, Christiane Schwieren and Judit Vall Castelló (2026)

Abstract

In this paper we study the potential of simple norm-based interventions to encourage help seeking for mental health problems. The target outcomes are mental-health stigma and help-seeking among young adults in Spain. Participants were randomly assigned to either a control group, an information treatment, a testimonial treatment, or a treatment combining information and testimonials. We find that testimonials from individuals with similar characteristics are the most effective intervention. Immediately after the intervention, the testimonial arm reduced self-stigma. At the one-month follow-up, the testimonial arm was associated with increased self-reported contact with psychologists and primary-care providers. By contrast, the effect of information alone is weak. The combined treatment does not yield clearly better outcomes than testimonials. Exploratory heterogeneity analyses suggest that some effects may be larger among female participants. The findings suggest that narratives from socially similar people are more effective than abstract norm information for changing attitudes and behavior.

From Gospel to Growth: Protestant Networks, Migration, and Development in Modern China

Abstract

This paper estimates the long-run effect of historical Protestant exposure on county-level economic development and migration in modern China. Using two independent measures of contemporary Protestant density across 2,089 counties and an instrumental-variables strategy based on historical events in the late Qing period, I find that counties with more Protestant presence are richer, more urbanised and more attractive to migrants today. The results suggest that Protestantism shaped long-run development partly through spatial networks that affect how people move and concentrate across regions.
